Supplementary Information for the 2027-2029 Immigration Levels Plan
Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) is pleased to release details of the 2027-2029 Immigration Levels Plan (Levels Plan). The Levels Plan includes temporary resident targets for new arrivals of international students and temporary workers, along with permanent resident targets and ranges under the immigration categories of Economic, Family, Refugees and Protected Persons, and Humanitarian & Compassionate and Other.
This year’s Levels Plan advances the Government’s commitment to sustainable immigration by building on progress already made to restore stability and predictability to the system. The Levels Plan aligns with the Government’s broader economic priorities by attracting the talent and skills needed to complement Canada’s domestic workforce and support major investments, while collaborating with provinces and territories, supporting regional and rural communities and reinforcing Canadians’ confidence in a well-managed immigration system.
Targets for new temporary resident arrivals will stabilize at 370,000 for all three years of the 2027-2029 Levels Plan. These figures represent new temporary worker and student arrivals. Temporary resident targets provide stability and predictability for provinces and territories, employers, and educational institutions while supporting short-term labour needs tied to major investments and helping Canada attract global talent. Workers are expected to represent approximately 60% of new arrivals over the course of the Plan, and students approximately 40%. For workers, targets are allocated under the International Mobility Program and the Temporary Foreign Worker Program.
Overall permanent resident admissions will remain at 380,000 for all three years of the 2027-2029 Levels Plan. As supporting the Canadian economy continues to be a key priority, the economic category remains the largest proportion of admissions each year at 64.4% across all three years. It supports a stronger focus on selecting the talent Canada needs to drive growth, support innovation, fill persistent labour shortages, and strengthen rural and remote communities while modestly increasing provincial programs to address regional labour needs.
This approach is supported by the continued reforms to Express Entry and federal economic programs to select more high-potential workers, including skilled tradespeople.
Targets for the Family, Refugees and Protected Persons, and Humanitarian & Compassionate and Other categories would remain stable, with the overall proportion of family class admissions remaining at 21.3% across all three years, 13% of overall admissions dedicated to Refugees and Protected Persons, and 1.3% of admissions to Humanitarian & Compassionate and Other to support Canada's obligations and humanitarian commitments.
The Plan reinforces Canada’s commitment to ensuring the vitality and economic development of Francophone Minority Communities, by setting targets for French-speaking permanent resident admissions outside Quebec at 9.5% in 2027, 10.5% in 2028, and 12% in 2029.
IRCC will also continue delivering existing one-time initiatives announced in Budget 2025 to accelerate the transition of up to 115,000 Protected Persons in Canada (PPiC) and their in-Canada dependants and 33,000 work permit holders to permanent residence in 2026 and 2027.
Time-Limited Measure
As implementation of the one-time initiatives continues, IRCC is also introducing a new time-limited measure to provide additional space to process permanent residence applications in 2027 within two cohorts:
First, the workers cohort will transition in-Canada workers and their accompanying family members to permanent residence in collaboration with provinces and territories in order to support regional labour market needs.
Second, the humanitarian cohort will transition individuals to permanent residence who are already in Canada and have been waiting for many years. This transition will provide fairness, keep families together, and fulfill some of Canada’s existing humanitarian commitments.
Given that this time-limited measure applies to people already living in Canada, transitioning them to permanent residence will deliver many benefits including improving the overall efficiency of Canada’s immigration system and supporting the economy, while not increasing pressure on services and infrastructure.

Targets for temporary residents include new arrivals under one of the temporary worker programs (i.e., the Temporary Foreign Worker Program (TFWP) and the International Mobility Program (IMP)), as well as new arrivals under the International Student Program (ISP).
